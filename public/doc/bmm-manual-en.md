# Transport, unpacking, set- up

## Transport

> Warning: For model with castors: when placing the machine in the truck, turn the levelling legs counterclockwise to release the castors, then secure the machine in place to prevent movement during transport.

### Loading & Unloading

A forklift (with minimum carrying capacity of 3000 kg) can be used for loading and unloading the machine. The frame is provided with slots for accommodating the forklift forks during loading and unloading. The machine's center of gravity lies between the two slots. For the machine's weight, see the Specifications chapter.

> Info: the machine can be equipped with lifting eyes that are specifically designed to allow the machine to be easily maneuvered into position.

> Warning: If possible, use a forklift to move the machine in its entirety. If the machine is to be hoisted and moved by a crane rail, use lifting eyes with the following specifications: M16, WLL 700 kg, CE- marked. Make sure the load bearing down on the eyes is distributed vertically: use a lifting beam to prevent lateral forces.

### Check for damage to machine and components

Before unloading, check whether damage has occurred during transport.

### Procedure for checking damage to the machine and/or components

In the event of damage, a claims form signed by the carrier must be submitted to Polymac BV. If the delivered machine and/or components are found to be in damaged condition after unpacking, please contact us.

## Unpacking

All Polymac machines are packed with due care and attention to ensure that no damage or defects occur.

To unpack:

1. Remove the bubble wrap from the main control box, function selection panel, guards and other parts of the machine.
1. Wipe the excess CRC SP 400 Soft Seal corrosion inhibitor off the metal parts with a clean, dry cloth.

Check that the unpacked parts are undamaged. If not, see 2.1.3.

The machine must be placed on a flat and level surface; no special foundation is required. If equipped with castors, the machine must rest on the levelling legs, not the castors. The castors are only required for moving the machine easily and quickly from one location to another. Make sure the machine is set up level on a flat surface.

# Intended purpose and conditions
## Intended use
The base machine is part of the modular bailer concept. This base in combination with a module is designed for a specific application, i.e. the forming of handles (metal/plastic) and attaching these handles on to plastic pails (for product specification, see  4.5.4) and stacking them.

Using the machine for other purposes may cause hazardous situations to occur or the machine to function improperly. In such cases, we advise you to contact Polymac.

## Operation of the machine

The Polymac base machine is designed to assemble handles onto a plastic pail. The assembled product is checked and stacked.

Plastic pails are fed into the machine by conveyor. The pails move into the machine one by one and are correctly positioned for assembly. The formed handle is moved in position for assembly and attached to the pail. Correct attachment is checked, incorrect assembled product exit the machine through a dedicated output. Correct products are stacked and complete stacks exit the machine by roller conveyor.

The forming of the handles (metal/plastic) is done in the module that is supplies separately.

# Placing and exchanging modules - Hoisting instructions

## Introduction

In order for the machine to produce stacks of pails with attached handles, a module needs to be placed on top of the base machine (BMM). Currently, there are four types of modules available:

- MH-BMF: Module for forming and presenting metal wires 
- MH-PBS: Module for forming and presenting flat plastic handles 
- MH-PBSB: Module for presenting preformed plastic handles 
- MH-PBM: Module for cutting and forming plastic handles from a mat and presenting these handles.

Underneath follows the procedure for placing and removing the module on the base machine BMM.

Please read these instructions carefully!

## Hoisting essentials

> Warning:
Warning
1. Hoisting and placing of the module may only be done by trained and qualified personnel
2. Personnel need to wear the prescribed personnel safety outfit and need to adhere to the lifting procedure described in this document.
3. Bystander not involved with the lifting need to stay in the prescribed safety zones, i.e. at least 5 m from the lifting area.
4. No persons are allowed to be present underneath the module while it is being lifted or hoisted!

## Placing the module - preparations

1. Make sure that there is enough space around the machine for hoisting and placing the module.
1. Make sure that the area around the machine is clean and free from litter in order to prevent persons tripping.
1. Make sure that the base machine is powered down.
1. Check whether the four hoisting bolts on top of the module are present and that these are tightened in the right way.
1. Use a hoisting chain, preferably a chain sling with four adjustable legs, that can accommodate the weight of the module. (See type plate of the module for module weight)
1. Adjust the length of the legs to accommodate for the center of gravity of the module. (To make sure that the module stays level while hoisting).
1. Affix the chain sling to the four hoisting bolts.

## Placing the module - hoisting

1. Hoist the module in a slow speed.
1. Check whether it stays level. If not, adjust the length of the legs of the chain sling.
1. It requires two persons to guide the module to the base machine.
> Warning: Use the grips on the module for guiding. Do not put your hands underneath the frame of the module!
1. Bring the module towards the base machine and align the module with the green guiding strips (See figure below).
1. Push the module in the guiding strips.
1. Slowly lower the module. Use the grips on the module to guide the module to the positioning cones. **Never put your hands underneath the module!**
1. Lower the module further until it rests on the positioning cones.
1. Detach the chain sling from the module.

# Safety
## Safety philosophy

Polymac BV complies with the safety strategy set forth in the Machinery Directive for reducing risks. This means: eliminate or reduce risks as far as possible by inherently safe design. In the latter case, there are residual risks and the most significant of these are set out in this manual and depicted via hazard pictograms on the machine.

The machine consists of moving parts that are not inherently safe by design due to the required processing speeds and corresponding acceleration and mass inertia forces. Therefore, the machine has a guard installed around it, with additional safety features at the required openings.
## EC Declaration of Incorporation regarding partly completed machinery
### Base

The machine falls within the scope of European product directives for machinery, low voltage and electromagnetic compatibility. As it concerns partly completed machinery, it does not have a CE marking. The content of the EC Declaration of Incorporation is listed in chapter 15.

The partly completed machinery may only be put into operation once it has been established that the machinery into which the partly completed machinery is to be incorporated complies with the provisions of the Machinery Directive (2006/42/EC).

## Safety measures and features

### Safety features at the outfeed openings

1. *Product infeed*
    This is the opening through which the pail goes into the machine. Because of the dimensions required for the pail, there is a (negligible) risk that this opening will be used as an access point to the machine from the conveyor side. However, the adjacent door serves as the access point.
1. *Stack of finished product outfeed*
    This is the opening through which finished products (stacks of pails) are discharged. Because of the dimensions required for the stacks, there is a (negligible) risk that this opening will be used as an access point to the machine from the conveyor side. However, the adjacent door serves as the access point.

Additional safety features (e.g. gate, light curtain) were considered but deemed impracticable and unlikely to deliver any significant increase in safety.

### Access control system

The access doors are monitored by a safety contact that is connected to a safety module. The doors open when a request for access is initiated by pressing the Open door' button. The button is fitted with a LED that flashes when the request is made, prompting the controller to put the machine in safe mode. When the machine is in safe mode, the LED will remain lit and the door may be opened. The safe mode is automatically terminated when the door is closed and the push button is pressed. If the door is opened without clearance from the access control system, an emergency stop occurs.

### Topside protection

The topside of the machine is open. Height of the machine and reach to the moving parts is sufficient to eliminate risk (in agreement with EN ISO 13857)

### Control cabinet protection

The electrical cabinet can only be opened when the main switch is set to '0'. The door can be unlocked with a special key. Boxes with control current can be opened with the special key.

### Disconnecting the power sources

To disconnect all power sources:

1. Turn off the compressed air supply by closing the main valve
1. Set the main switch of the control box to 0 and lock it with a padlock.

## General terms
- Equipment module
    Modules in the machine representing physical modules: (Baseplate, conveyor, centring, handle station, Hydraulic, etc.). Each Equipment module consists of one or more Control modules.
- Control module
    Control modules are groups of actuators and sensors that perform a certain function in the machine. Examples are cylinders, lifts and axes (servos).
- End feedback
    Feedback sensor on the end of a cylinder stroke (Note: when the cylinder is on his activated position)
- Start feedback
    Feedback sensor on the begin of a cylinder stroke (Note: when the cylinder is on his rest/deactivated position)
- Unlocked
    There is no cylinder that blocks the stroke from a cylinder that is unlocked
- Activate
    Cylinder start moving to the end position
- Deactivate
    Cylinder start moving to the start position(rest position)
- Activate time
    Time that is used when there is no end feedback available. It gives a delay so the cylinder has enough time to reach his end position before the next step is started.
- De-activate time
    Time that is used when there is no start feedback available. It gives a delay so the cylinder has enough time to reach his start position before the next step is started.
- On/Off delay time
    Extra time to create a delay before a cylinder will be activated / deactivated

## Lubrication

### Linear Systems

Proper lubrication is essential to the efficient performance of the linear system. Improper lubrication will result not only in increased risk of wear, but also in significant shortening of the service life.

Lubrication:

1. Reduces wear and friction resistance, such as internal erosion of the moving parts.
1. Creates an even coating of grease on the conveyor belts, reducing the load pressure and extending the service life. 
1. Protects metal surfaces against corrosion.

To avoid compromising the linear system's performance and maintaining the lubrication over a prolonged period of time, the lubricant used must be compatible with the ambient conditions and meet all specific requirements.

In general, a lubrication interval of \(5000km\) operating capacity or a minimum of once per 6 months must be observed. This capacity may be lower under special ambient conditions. Relevant factors may include:

1. Extreme operating temperatures;
1. Influence of condensation and splash water;
1. High vibration load;
1. Deposit from certain substances (acids, hydrocarbons);
1. High dynamic use;
1. Permanently small stroke movements.

Lubrication of the linear system is performed with the grease nipple on the linear carriage and a suitable grease gun. Use a grease gun with a known discharge rate (for example THK MG70) and the correct type of grease. Note that too much grease results in too much pressure in the bearing block, causing premature failure. Consult the lubrication table of the recommended amount and the maintenance schedule for the intervals.

> Info: Grease Type: Lithium based EP2 grease (for example THK AFB-LF Grease).
    Too much grease can cause premature failure

1. Shell: Tivela Oil WB

### Lubrication of drum motors (if present)

The drum motors are provided with oil. The first oil replenishment/change is recommended after 200- 300 operating hours, each further change after 10,000 operating hours. The oil specification is 100cST/40°C or 8\~ngler/50°C. The oil is suitable for ambient temperatures from \(- 20^{\circ}C\) to \(+50^{\circ}C\) . If another type of oil is used, make sure that it does not contain any additives that may damage the motor insulation. ## Timing belts (if applicable)

The machine is equipped with timing belts for performing a variety of movements. The timing belt drives must be regularly inspected.

![alt text](image-98.png)

### Checklist for maintenance of timing belts

1. Switch off the drive.
1. Place all machine parts in a safe (neutral) position
1. Check the belt for wear or damage. Replace if necessary. See Installation of timing belts.
1. Check the disks and gears, and replace if worn or damaged.
1. Also check other drive components such as bearings, shafts, mounting points, etc.
1. Check belt tension and adjust if necessary.
1. Recheck alignment of disks.
1. Connect the machine and let the drive run. Look and listen for abnormalities.

### Installation of timing belts
1. After switching off the machine and locking the main switch, disconnect the attachment points in the motor or the tensioner. Remove the belt without bending or twisting it. Do not twist a belt off the disk!
1. Remove and inspect the old belts. Abnormal or excessive wear may indicate improper maintenance.
1. Choose the proper replacement belt. (Refer to the mechanical parts list).
1. Clean the disks and tensioners with a cloth that is slightly moistened with a mild, non-volatile solvent. Do not use a sharp object to remove grease or dirt from the belt. Belts must be fitted dry onto the drive.
1. Check the gears for extraordinary or excessive wear. Also check the alignment. Correct alignment is particularly critical in synchronous belts.
1. Check the other drive components. Are bearings and shafts properly aligned, well lubricated? Are they worn out?
1. Install the new belt on the gears. Do not twist or use force to mount it.
1. Adjust the belt until the tension gauge shows the correct tension. Turn the drives a few revolutions by hand and check the tension again.
1. Properly tighten the bolts of the mounting points in the motor or the tensioner. All drive components must be firmly secured in place because any change to the center distance compromises the drive's performance.
1. Although the belts do not require re- tensioning, we recommend that you let the drive run and observe it carefully. Look and listen for unusual noises and vibrations. Switch off the machine and check the bearings and motor. If they feel hot to the touch, the belt may be too tight, or the bearings may be misaligned or improperly lubricated.

## Operation of the machine

The Polymac MH- PBSB module is designed to take plastic handles from a magazine and present these handles to a handle transport in the base machine to be assembled onto a plastic pail.
